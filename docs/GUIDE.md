# Duckfoot Photo — Help Guide

This guide is for anyone who wants to make their photos look better. You don't need to
know anything about cameras or computers beyond opening a file. Every section here is
also inside the app: click **Help** in the top bar.

## Welcome

Duckfoot Photo lets you open a photo, make it look the way you remember, and save a copy
to share.

**Your original photo is never changed.** Everything you do is kept as a list of
instructions beside the photo, so you can always go back to how it came out of the camera.

The window has five parts:

- **Top bar**: open photos, undo and redo, and **Export** to save a finished copy.
- **Photo**: the big picture in the middle.
- **Filmstrip**: the row of small pictures under the photo. Click one to open it.
- **Editing panel**: everything on the right. This is where you change how the photo looks.
- **Shelf**: the thin strip of icons on the far left, for your list of photos, folders,
  snapshots and history.

New here? Click **Take the tour** in Help. It points at each part in turn and takes about
a minute.

## Your first edit

1. Click **Open RAW…** in the top bar and choose a photo. (Any photo works, not just RAW
   files.) You can also drag photos from a folder onto the window.
2. Look at the editing panel on the right. It starts on the **Basic** tab.
3. Drag the **exposure** slider to the right to brighten the photo, or left to darken it.
4. If the colours look too blue or too orange, move **temp** under **white balance**.
5. Not sure? Press **Ctrl+Z** to undo. Double-click any slider to put it back where it started.
6. When you like it, click **Export** in the top bar to save a copy you can share.

That's it. Your edits are saved on their own as you go.

## Bringing photos in

- **Open RAW…** in the top bar opens one photo.
- **Drag and drop**: drag photos from your file manager onto the Duckfoot window. They are
  added to your shelf, and the first one opens.
- **The + on the left strip** adds more photos to the shelf.
- **The folder icon on the left strip** lets you browse a folder and load every photo in it.

Duckfoot opens camera RAW files from almost every camera (Canon, Nikon, Sony, Fujifilm,
Olympus, Panasonic, Pentax and more), plus ordinary JPEG, PNG, TIFF and WebP pictures, and
iPhone photos (HEIC). If a file
won't open, the bar at the bottom of the window says which file and why.

## Choosing and sorting photos

The filmstrip under the photo shows every photo on your shelf. Click a picture to open it.
The **left and right arrow keys** step to the previous or next photo.

To find the good ones quickly:

- **Stars**: press **1** to **5** to give the open photo a rating, or **0** to clear it.
  The stars in the filmstrip's top line show only photos rated that high or higher.
- **Flags**: press **P** to mark a photo as a *pick* (a keeper) or **X** to *reject* it.
  **U** clears the flag. The **Flags** buttons show picks only, hide rejects, and so on.
- **Colour labels**: press **6**, **7**, **8** or **9** for red, yellow, green or blue.
  The coloured dots show only photos with that label.
- **Search** finds photos by file name or keyword.
- **Sort** puts them in order by name, rating or size.

To pick several photos at once, hold **Ctrl** and click each one, or click one and
**Shift**-click another to select everything between them.

## The editing panel

Everything that changes how the photo looks is on the right, sorted into tabs:

- **Basic**: brightness, colour temperature, shadows and highlights, how vivid the colours are.
- **Tone**: contrast, the tone curve, clarity and haze.
- **Colour**: change one colour at a time (make the sky bluer, say), colour grading, and looks.
- **Detail**: sharpening and noise reduction for grainy low-light photos.
- **Optics**: crop and straighten, lens corrections, and darkening or lightening the corners.
- **Retouch**: brush on changes in one area, and remove spots or dust.
- **All**: every tool in one long list, folded so you open one at a time.

Each tool is a box with a **tick box** on the left. A tick means it is switched on. Moving
any of its sliders switches it on for you. Untick it to see the photo without that tool.
The number beside a tab name tells you how many of its tools are on.

**Sliders:**

- Drag to change. The photo updates as you drag.
- Hold **Shift** while dragging for finer control.
- **Double-click** a slider to reset it.
- The number on the right shows the exact value. Double-click it to type a number.

## Choosing which tools show

Too many tools? Click the **gear** (⚙) beside *Pipeline* in the editing panel. There you can:

- Untick tools you never use, so they disappear from every tab.
- Move tools up or down to change their order.
- Click **Essentials** to show only the handful most people need, or **Show all** to bring
  everything back.
- Change the **text size** of the whole app.

Click the gear again to go back to editing. This only changes what you *see*. Hidden
tools still work if they were already switched on.

## Cropping and straightening

1. Click **Crop** above the photo, or press **C**.
2. Drag the corners or edges to frame the picture. Drag inside the frame to move it.
3. Pick a shape (*1:1* for a square, *4:5*, *3:2*, *16:9* and so on) from the buttons
   that appear, or press **X** to turn the shape on its side.
4. Press **Enter** to keep the crop, or **Esc** to cancel.

To straighten a tilted horizon, use **Straighten** under **crop & rotate** on the
**Optics** tab. **[** and **]** turn the photo a quarter turn left or right. **H** mirrors it.

## Comparing before and after

Above the photo:

- **A / B** splits the photo so you can see the original and your edit side by side. Drag
  the line to move it. Press **\\** to switch it on or off.
- **Clip** shows any parts that are pure white (in red) or pure black (in blue), where the
  detail is lost. Press **J** to switch it on or off.
- **Proof** shows how the colours will look in the file you export.
- **Fit** shows the whole photo. **1:1** zooms in to see every detail. You can also scroll
  over the photo to zoom, and drag to move around.

## Starting from a look

Not sure where to begin? Open **Looks** in the editing panel (below the tools) and pick a
style: *vivid*, *warm*, *film*, *mono*, *noir* and others. A look is just a starting
point. Change any slider afterwards, or undo it with **Ctrl+Z**.

## Undo, snapshots and history

- **Undo** and **Redo** are the curved arrows in the top bar (**Ctrl+Z** and
  **Ctrl+Shift+Z**). Every photo has its own undo list, so going back on one photo never
  touches another.
- **History**: open the shelf (the photos icon on the far left) to see every change you
  made to this photo. Click a line to jump back to that moment.
- **Snapshots**: also in the shelf. A snapshot keeps a version you like, under a name, so
  you can try something else and come back to it.
- **Revert**, under the editing panel, goes back to the last time the photo was saved.

## Reusing your edits

Shot a whole set in the same light? Edit one, then reuse it. These buttons are at the
bottom of the editing panel:

- **Copy** remembers this photo's edits. **Paste** puts them on the photo that's open.
- **Modules…** chooses *which* edits Copy and Paste carry. For example, you might copy
  the colour but not the exposure.
- **Previous** gives this photo the same edits as the photo you had open before it.
- **Paste to all** puts the copied edits on every photo on the shelf. If you change your
  mind, the same button becomes **Undo paste**.
- **Reset all** clears every edit on this photo.

Pasting never changes a photo's crop or rotation. Each photo keeps its own framing.

## Saving your work

**You don't need to save.** Duckfoot saves your edits by itself a moment after you stop
changing things. **Ctrl+S** (or **Save sidecar**) saves straight away if you'd like to be sure.

Your edits are kept in a small file next to your photo, with the same name. That small
file is what Duckfoot calls a *sidecar*. Your original photo is never written to. Keep
the two files together if you move them.

**Save sidecar** and **Revert** are at the bottom of the editing panel.

## Exporting a finished photo

Exporting makes a new picture file you can share, print or post.

1. Click **Export** in the top bar, or press **Ctrl+E**.
2. Choose the **size**, the **format** and whether to include details like the camera and
   location. **JPEG** is right for almost everything: email, websites, phones and printing.
3. Choose where to save it.

The line above **Save sidecar** shows what an export will make. Click it to change the settings.

**Many photos at once:** select them in the filmstrip (Ctrl+click), then click
**Export Batch**. You can save several versions of each (a large one and a small one, for
example) and choose how the new files are named.

## Cutting out a background

**Cutout** in the top bar opens Cutout Studio. It finds the main subject (a person or a
product, say) and removes the background around it. Touch up the edge if needed, then
save it as a PNG with a see-through or a plain white background.

## Keyboard shortcuts

Press **?** at any time to see every shortcut. The handful worth learning first:

- **Ctrl+Z** / **Ctrl+Shift+Z**: undo / redo
- **Left** / **Right** arrows: previous / next photo
- **1**–**5**: star rating. **P**: pick. **X**: reject.
- **C**: crop
- **\\**: before / after
- **Ctrl+E**: export

Shortcuts don't fire while you're typing in a box, such as Search.

## If something goes wrong

- **A photo won't open.** The bar at the bottom of the window gives the file name and a
  reason. Some very new or unusual cameras aren't supported yet.
- **Everything looks wrong after an edit.** Press **Ctrl+Z** until it's right, or click
  **Reset all** at the bottom of the editing panel.
- **A tool is missing.** It may be hidden. Click the gear (⚙) in the editing panel and
  tick it, or click **Show all**.
- **The text is too small.** Click the gear (⚙) and choose a larger **text size**.
- **The app feels slow.** Very large photos take a moment to open the first time. The next
  photo in the filmstrip is prepared while you look at this one, so stepping forward is quick.
